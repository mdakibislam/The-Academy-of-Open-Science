# Introduction to Mathematics

Mathematics is the study of patterns, structure, change, and space. It is not merely a tool for calculation or a set of memorized rules—it is an absolute, self-consistent language built on formal logic.

Where empirical sciences like physics or biology rely on experimental observation to establish truth, mathematics relies on **deductive reasoning from first principles**. Once a mathematical truth is proved, it remains true universally and indefinitely.

## 1. What is Mathematics?

At its core, mathematics operates on three primary components:

1. **Axioms (Postulates):** Fundamental, self-evident assumptions that are accepted without proof (e.g., "Things that are equal to the same thing are equal to one another").
2. **Definitions:** Precise statements defining abstract concepts, mathematical objects, and operators (e.g., prime numbers, sets, functions).
3. **Theorems & Proofs:** Logical deductions that follow strictly from axioms and definitions. A theorem is a statement proven to be true; the proof is the logical chain that establishes it.

$$\text{Axioms} + \text{Definitions} \xrightarrow{\text{Deductive Logic}} \text{Theorems}$$

## 2. Why Study Math from First Principles?

Most standard curricula teach mathematics procedurally: students learn *how* to apply a formula to get an answer on a test. 

The Academy of Open Science takes a **first-principles approach**. By building every concept from scratch, you learn *why* a formula exists and *how* it was discovered. Understanding the foundation gives you the power to:
* Re-derive equations independently if you forget them.
* Recognize hidden mathematical patterns in real-world systems.
* Solve novel problems that do not fit standard textbook templates.

## 3. The Core Branches of Mathematics

Mathematics is generally divided into two main categories: **Pure Mathematics** (studying structures for their internal logic) and **Applied Mathematics** (using mathematical structures to model physical reality).
